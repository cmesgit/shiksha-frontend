Natural Vegetation
& Wildlife

From dense tropical rainforests to alpine meadows — explore India's extraordinary biodiversity, the five forest types, and the wildlife that calls them home.

📚 NCERT Class 9🗺️ Contemporary India — I⏱️ 14 min read

India is one of the world's twelve megabiodiversity countries — home to over 47,000 plant species (10th in the world, 4th in Asia) and 89,000 animal species, making it one of the richest natural heritage nations on Earth.

This extraordinary variety is shaped by India's diverse relief, climate, soils, and geography — from the icy Himalayas to tropical coasts, each zone nurturing its own distinct community of life.

Key Concepts

Important Definitions

Natural Vegetation

A plant community that grows naturally without human help and has remained undisturbed for a long time.

Virgin Vegetation

Natural vegetation that has been left undisturbed by humans for a long time. If purely Indian, it is called endemic; if from outside, it is called exotic.

Flora

The plants of a specific region or time period. India has over 47,000 plant species, including 15,000 flowering plants.

Fauna

The animal species of a specific region. India has 89,000 species of animals and rich freshwater and marine fish varieties.

Determining Factors

What Shapes Flora & Fauna?

🏔️ Relief Factors

  • Nature of land — mountains, plains, plateaus
  • Dry vs wet regions affect plant types
  • Different soil types — sandy, deltaic, rocky — support different vegetation
  • Altitude determines the succession of vegetation belts

🌦️ Climatic Factors

  • Temperature — determines vegetation from tropical to alpine
  • Photoperiod — duration of sunlight varies growth; trees grow faster in summer
  • Precipitation — heavy rainfall = dense vegetation; sparse rainfall = scrublands
  • Humidity in the air also significantly affects plant character
🌐

Ecosystem & Biome

An ecosystem is the system where all plants and animals in an area are interdependent and interrelated to each other and their physical environment. Human beings are an integral part of it.

A biome is a very large ecosystem on land with a distinct type of vegetation and animal life. The greed of human beings leads to overutilisation of resources — threatening these ecosystems.

Practice Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What is natural vegetation? Distinguish between flora and fauna.

Natural vegetation refers to a plant community that grows naturally without human help and has remained undisturbed for a long time. Flora refers to plants of a specific region, while fauna refers to the animal species of that region. Vegetation that has been left undisturbed for a very long time is called virgin vegetation. Native Indian plant species are called endemic species; those that arrived from outside India are called exotic plants.

Q2. What are the main factors that determine natural vegetation in a region?

Relief factors: The nature of land — mountains, plains, plateaus, dry/wet regions — affects vegetation. Different soil types (sandy, deltaic, etc.) support different plants.

Climatic factors: Three main climatic factors determine vegetation — temperature (determines tropical to alpine types), photoperiod (sunlight duration affects growth rates — trees grow faster in summer), and precipitation (heavy rainfall areas have denser vegetation).

Q3. What is an ecosystem? What is a biome?

An ecosystem is a system where all plants and animals in an area are interdependent and interrelated to each other and their physical environment. Human beings are an integral part of the ecosystem. A biome is a very large ecosystem on land having a distinct type of vegetation and animal life. Examples include the tropical rainforest biome, the grassland biome, and the desert biome.

Q4. Describe Tropical Evergreen Rainforests of India.

Found in areas with more than 200 cm of rainfall and a warm, wet climate all year round. Characterised by luxurious trees, shrubs, and creepers. Trees have no fixed time to shed leaves, so forests appear evergreen throughout the year. Located in the Western Ghats, island groups, upper Assam, and Tamil Nadu. Animals found include elephants, monkeys, lemurs, and a large variety of birds, bats, sloth, and reptiles.

Q5. What are Tropical Deciduous Forests and how are they divided?

Also called Monsoon Forests, these are the most widespread forests in India. Rainfall ranges between 70–200 cm. Trees shed their leaves for about 6–8 weeks in the dry season.

They are divided into: Moist Deciduous (rainfall 100–200 cm — found in northeast states, foothills of Himalayas, Jharkhand, West Odisha, Chhattisgarh, eastern slopes of Western Ghats) and Dry Deciduous (rainfall 70–100 cm — found in rainier parts of the peninsula and plains). Animals: lions, tigers, pigs, deer, elephants, birds, reptiles.

Q6. Describe Tropical Thorn Forests and Scrubs.

Found where rainfall is less than 70 cm — the northwest part of the country and semi-arid areas of Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, UP, and Haryana. Plants have thick, small leaves to minimise evaporation and succulent stems to conserve water. Trees are widely spaced with thorny branches. Animals include rats, mice, rabbits, fox, wolf, tiger, and lion.

Q7. What are Montane Forests? Describe the vegetation belts with altitude.

Montane forests show a succession of natural vegetation belts with increasing altitude — similar to the change from tropical to tundra regions.

Belt progression: Tropical forests at the base → Subtropical forests (1,000–2,000 m) → Temperate forests (2,000–3,600 m) → Temperate grasslands (above 3,600 m) → Alpine vegetation (higher altitudes) → Tundra vegetation at the snow line. Animals include yaks, snow leopard, chiru, and Tibetan antelope in the Himalayas.

Q8. What are Mangrove Forests? Where are they found in India?

Mangrove forests are found in tidal areas along coasts — areas influenced by tides. They feature dense mangroves with roots submerged underwater. Found in the delta regions of major rivers — especially the Ganga–Brahmaputra delta (Sundarbans). Also found along the coasts of Odisha, West Bengal, and the Andaman & Nicobar Islands. The famous Royal Bengal Tiger is the iconic animal of these forests.

Q9. What makes India's wildlife exceptional?

India has over 89,000 species of animals, 1,200 species of birds, and 2,500 species of fish. It accounts for 5–8% of the world's amphibians, reptiles, and mammals.

India is the only country in the world that has both tigers and lions. The Himalayas have animals adapted to extreme cold. India is one of the twelve megabiodiversity countries of the world.

Q10. What steps has the government taken to conserve flora and fauna?

The government has set up 14 Biosphere Reserves to protect flora and fauna in their natural ecosystems. It has also established 89 National Parks and 49 Wildlife Sanctuaries to protect natural heritage. Various conservation programmes target specific endangered species like tigers (Project Tiger), elephants (Project Elephant), and crocodiles. Hunting and pollution remain the biggest threats, and legal frameworks like the Wildlife Protection Act have been enacted to check them.
