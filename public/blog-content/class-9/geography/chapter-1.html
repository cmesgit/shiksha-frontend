and Location

📚 NCERT Class 9🗺️ Contemporary India — I⏱️ 12 min read

India is one of the world's oldest civilisations and today stands as a rapidly developing nation making remarkable progress across multiple fields.

Geographically, India occupies a unique and central position — straddling the ancient land and maritime routes between East and West. It lies entirely in the Northern Hemisphere, with the Tropic of Cancer dividing it into two nearly equal halves.

With an area of 3.28 million km², India ranks as the 7th largest country in the world — and is the only nation to have an ocean named after it.

Also spans ~30 degrees. Appears smaller on maps because longitudes converge at higher latitudes.

☀️

Tropic of Cancer — 23°30'N

Passes almost through the middle of India, dividing it into two nearly equal halves — the northern temperate zone and the southern tropical zone. It passes through Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram.

🕐

Standard Meridian — 82°30'E (IST)

India's Standard Time is based on 82°30'E, passing near Mirzapur, Uttar Pradesh. India is 5 hrs 30 min ahead of GMT. A single standard time was needed because India's 30° longitudinal span would otherwise create significant time differences between east and west.

India & The World

Strategic Position in Asia

🛣️ Land Routes

  • India's contacts with the world via land routes are much older than its maritime contacts
  • Himalayan passes enabled exchange with Central Asia, Persia, and China
  • The Indian landmass is centrally located between West and East Asia

🚢 Maritime Routes

  • The trans-Indian Ocean routes connect the West with the East
  • India's Deccan Peninsula protrudes into the ocean — close contact with Africa, West Asia, Southeast Asia & Europe
  • India's peninsular shape makes it a natural hub for maritime trade
Cultural Exchange

What India Gave & Received

🟢 India Gave to the World

  • Philosophies: Upanishads, Ramayana, Panchtantra
  • Mathematics: Indian numerals and the decimal system
  • Spread of Buddhism and Jainism across Asia
  • Ayurveda, astronomy, and early scientific traditions

🟠 India Received from the World

  • Greek sculpture influenced Indian art and architectural forms
  • Architectural styles from West Asia shaped Indo-Islamic and Mughal architecture
  • Persian and Arabic cultural and linguistic influences

The Palk Strait

Sri Lanka is separated from India by the Palk Strait — a narrow strip of sea between the two nations. Despite this separation, India and Sri Lanka share millennia-old cultural, religious, and trade ties.

Practice Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Where is India located? Describe its latitudinal and longitudinal extent.

India lies entirely in the Northern Hemisphere. Its mainland extends between 8°4'N to 37°6'N latitudes and 68°7'E to 97°25'E longitudes. Both the latitudinal and longitudinal extent is approximately 30 degrees.

Q2. What is the significance of the Tropic of Cancer passing through India?

The Tropic of Cancer (23°30'N) passes almost through the middle of India, dividing it into two nearly equal halves. The northern part lies in the temperate zone while the southern part lies in the tropical zone — giving India a diverse climate.

Q3. What is India's Standard Meridian and why was it chosen?

India's Standard Time is based on the 82°30'E meridian, which passes near Mirzapur in Uttar Pradesh. It was chosen as it lies centrally with respect to India's longitudinal extent. India is 5 hours and 30 minutes ahead of GMT.

Q4. Why does India's east-west extent appear smaller than the north-south extent?

Although both extents span approximately 30 degrees, the east-west extent appears smaller because longitudes converge as we move towards the poles. At higher latitudes, the actual distance between two longitudes is less than at the equator.

Q5. Describe India's size and its position among world countries.

India covers an area of 3.28 million square kilometres, which is 2.4% of the total geographical area of the world. It is the 7th largest country in the world. It has a land boundary of about 15,200 km and a total coastline of 7,516.6 km.

Q6. How does India's central location benefit it in terms of world trade routes?

India's central location between West and East Asia gives it a strategic advantage. The trans-Indian Ocean routes connect countries of Europe in the west with those of East and Southeast Asia in the east. India's protruding Deccan Peninsula extends into the Indian Ocean, allowing close contact with West Asia, Africa, Europe, and Southeast Asia.

Q7. Name India's neighbouring countries. Which ones share a land boundary and which share a sea boundary?

Land boundary neighbours: Pakistan, Afghanistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, and Myanmar.

Sea boundary neighbours: Maldives and Sri Lanka — both island countries to the south. Sri Lanka is separated from India by the Palk Strait.

Q8. What contributions has India made to the world? What did India receive in return?

India's contributions: Ideas and philosophies (Upanishads, Ramayana, Panchtantra), and mathematics (Indian numerals and the decimal system).

India received: Influence on its architecture from Greek sculpture and architectural styles from West Asia.

Q9. Why does India stand apart from the rest of Asia?

India is separated from the rest of Asia by the mighty Himalayas to the north and the seas on three sides — the Arabian Sea (west), the Bay of Bengal (east), and the Indian Ocean (south). This natural boundary makes India a distinct sub-continent.

Q10. What is unique about India among all the countries in the world?

India is the only country in the world that has an ocean named after it — the Indian Ocean. This reflects India's historical maritime importance and central position in ocean trade routes.
