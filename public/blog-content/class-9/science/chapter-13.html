Why Do We Fall Ill? explains the difference between health and disease, the causes of illness, how diseases spread, how the body defends itself and how diseases can be prevented.

The chapter is important because it connects biology with daily life: hygiene, nutrition, immunity, vaccination, public health and infectious agents.

01
Basic Concepts

Health, Disease, Symptoms and Signs

Well-being

Health

Health is a state of physical, mental and social well-being. It includes unity and harmony within the mind, body and soul of an organism.

Disease is any condition that can lead to discomfort, distress, health problems and even death of the affected person.

04
Spread Based

Infectious and Non-infectious Diseases

I

Infectious / Communicable

Diseases caused by infectious agents such as bacteria, viruses, protozoans and fungi. They can spread from an infected person to a healthy person.

Examples: influenza, common cold, dengue, cholera, typhoid.
N

Non-infectious / Non-communicable

Diseases caused by internal causes such as excessive weight, genetic defects, nutrient deficiency, organ malfunction or bad habits.

Examples: high blood pressure, cancer, diabetes, deficiency diseases.
05
Infectious Agents

Microbes that Cause Diseases

Viruses

Tiny organisms that grow, multiply or reproduce only inside host cells.

Influenza, cold, dengue, AIDS, SARS

Bacteria

Unicellular organisms, larger than viruses. Some are harmful while some are beneficial.

Typhoid, cholera, whooping cough, anthrax

Fungi

Plant-like, eukaryotic, heterotrophic organisms.

Athlete’s foot, candidiasis, ringworms

Protozoa

Simple, primitive unicellular organisms often found in water.

Amoebiasis, kala azar, malaria

Worms

Multicellular parasites that may infect intestines of humans and animals.

Diarrhoea, anaemia, liver rot

Mode of Transmission

How Communicable Diseases Spread

💨

Air-borne

Spread through coughing, sneezing, talking and droplets in air.

Cold, chicken pox, small pox, pneumonia, influenza, tuberculosis
💧

Water-borne

Spread when excretions from infected persons mix with drinking water.

Cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A
🍽

Food-borne

Caused by consuming food infected by disease-causing microbes.

Botulism, stomach infections
🤝

Contact

Spread by contact with a diseased person or articles used by them.

Swine flu, ringworm, conjunctivitis
🦟

Vectors

Animals that transfer microorganisms from infected persons to others.

Female mosquito transfers malaria-causing Plasmodium
Other transmission routes: Physical contact may include sexually transmitted diseases such as syphilis, gonorrhoea and AIDS. Blood-to-blood contact can occur through blood transfusion or during pregnancy between mother and baby.

09
Body Response

Effects of Diseases and Defence Mechanisms

Local Effects

Effects such as swelling, pain and joint stiffness occur only at the site of infection.

General Effects

Effects such as fever, chills, headache, fatigue and loss of appetite occur all over the body.

Inflammation

Inflammation is the process by which the immune system responds to protect the body from infection.

Specific Defence Mechanism

Our body resists microbes through general and specific mechanisms. Specific defence targets particular pathogens and antigens.

White blood cells help defend the body.
Monocytes mature into macrophages, which engulf invading pathogens.
Lymphocytes generate antibodies against antigens.
Specific antibodies prevent pathogen entry into cells or kill pathogens.
Ordinary substances like pollen, dust, fruits or vegetables may act as antigens and cause allergies.
10
Control and Care

Treatment and Prevention

Treatment works in two main ways.

Diseases may be treated by reducing their effects or by killing the actual cause of the disease.

Reducing the effect

This approach reduces discomfort, fever, pain or other effects produced by the disease.

Killing the cause

This approach targets the disease-causing agent responsible for illness.

General Ways

✓
Stay away from the diseased person.
✓
Ensure safe drinking water supply.
✓
Maintain a clean environment to prevent vectors like mosquitoes from breeding.
✓
Cover your mouth and nose while coughing or sneezing.
✓
Ensure proper nutrition so the immune system functions properly.

Specific Ways

✓
Vaccination: Protection by administering agents that mimic microbes.
✓
Vaccines are available against tetanus, polio, measles, hepatitis B, whooping cough and yellow fever.
✓
Vaccines can be prepared from dead germs, weakened germs, virulent germs or toxoids.
✓
Immunization: Strengthening the immune system to fight microbes.

Personal Hygiene

Includes cleanliness of the body, drinking clean water and maintaining habits that reduce infection risk.

Public Hygiene

Includes cleaning water reservoirs, proper disposal of sewage and maintaining public cleanliness.

Quick FAQ Revision

What is health?
Health is a state of physical, mental and social well-being. It includes harmony within the mind, body and soul of an organism.
What is disease?
Disease is any condition that can lead to discomfort, distress, health problems and even death of the affected person.
What is the difference between symptoms and signs of disease?
Symptoms are indications felt by the patient, such as headache, stomach pain and nausea. Signs are observations such as fever, vomiting and diarrhoea that can be noticed by a physician.
What is incubation period?
Incubation period is the time interval between infection and the appearance of symptoms.
What are contributory causes of disease?
Contributory causes include unhealthy conditions, improper public services and poverty. These conditions make disease more likely.
What are immediate causes of disease?
Immediate causes are external causes like microorganisms. Viruses, bacteria and other microorganisms can directly cause disease.
What is the difference between acute and chronic disease?
Acute diseases last for a short period, such as cold, cough and influenza. Chronic diseases last for a long period, such as diabetes and kidney stones.
What are infectious diseases?
Infectious diseases are caused by infectious agents such as bacteria, viruses, protozoans and fungi. They can spread from an infected person to a healthy person.
What are non-infectious diseases?
Non-infectious diseases are caused by internal causes such as excessive weight or genetic defects. Examples include high blood pressure and cancer.
Name some diseases caused by viruses.
Diseases caused by viruses include influenza, common cold caused by Rhinovirus, dengue, AIDS and SARS.
Name some diseases caused by bacteria.
Bacterial diseases include whooping cough, typhoid, cholera, anthrax, pneumonia, dysentery, plague and diphtheria.
What causes malaria?
Malaria is caused by Plasmodium. The malarial parasite requires two hosts: humans and female Anopheles mosquito.
What are vectors?
Vectors are animals that transfer disease-causing microorganisms from an infected person to others. Female mosquitoes can transfer malaria-causing Plasmodium.
What are local and general effects of disease?
Local effects include swelling, pain and joint stiffness at the site of infection. General effects include fever, chills, headache, fatigue and loss of appetite.
What is inflammation?
Inflammation is the process by which the body’s immune system responds to protect the body from infection.
What are specific defence mechanisms?
Specific defence includes engulfing pathogens by white blood cells and producing specific antibodies against specific antigens.
What is vaccination?
Vaccination protects the body from communicable diseases by administering agents that mimic the microbe. Vaccines are available against diseases such as tetanus, polio, measles, hepatitis B and whooping cough.
How can infectious diseases be prevented?
They can be prevented through personal hygiene, public hygiene, safe drinking water, clean environment, proper nutrition, covering the mouth and nose while coughing or sneezing and vaccination.
