Every motion you see around you — a ball rolling, a door opening, water falling — is caused by force. Force is the reason objects move, stop, change shape, or change direction.

This chapter explores what force is, how forces add and subtract, their effects on objects, the types of forces (contact and non-contact), and the concept of pressure — why a knife cuts but a palm doesn't.

Calculation

Net Force — Combining Forces

When multiple forces act on an object, the combined effect is the net force. Direction determines whether forces add or subtract:

➡️ Same Direction — Forces ADD UP
F = F₁ + F₂
↔️ Opposite Directions — Forces SUBTRACT
F = |F₁ − F₂|

What Force Does

Effects of Force

An external force acting on an object can produce three different effects:

🫓
Change Shape & Size
Example: Rolling dough ball into a flat chapati changes its shape.
🛒
Change State of Motion
Example: Pushing a stationary cart makes it move (rest → motion).
🏏
Change Speed & Direction
Example: A batsman hitting a cricket ball changes its speed and direction.

Types of Forces

Contact & Non-Contact Forces

🤝
Contact Forces
Forces that require physical touching/contact between objects to act.
💪 Muscular Force
Force applied due to the action of muscles. Example: lifting, pushing objects.
🔀 Frictional Force
Force that opposes relative motion between surfaces in contact.
🌌
Non-Contact Forces
Forces that act even without objects physically touching each other.
🧲 Magnetic Force
Force exerted by a magnet on another magnet or iron piece.
⚡ Electrostatic Force
Force by a charged object on charged or uncharged objects.
🌍 Gravitational Force
Force of attraction between any two objects having mass.

Force per Unit Area

Pressure — Formula & Concept

Formula
Force (N)
Area (m²)
SI Unit: Pascal (Pa) = N/m²
↑
Pressure increases when area decreases (same force on smaller area)
↓
Pressure decreases when area increases (same force on larger area)
↑
Pressure increases when force is increased on same area
🔩 Why Pointed Nail Goes in Easier — Pressure Concept

💡 Key Rule: Pressure increases when the area decreases and the applied force is increased. This is why knife blades, needles, and pointed nails are designed with sharp, small-area tips — to maximise pressure with minimum force.

6
Fluids

Pressure in Liquids, Gases & Atmosphere

Liquids and gases exert pressure on the walls of their containers in all directions. Two factors determine how much pressure they exert:

📏
Factor 1: Depth
Pressure exerted by a liquid or gas increases with depth. The deeper you go, the greater the pressure. Example: Dam walls are made thicker at the bottom to withstand higher water pressure at greater depth.
⚖️
Factor 2: Density
Pressure increases with the density of the liquid or gas. A denser fluid exerts more pressure at the same depth compared to a less dense fluid.
🏛️ Why Dam Walls Are Thicker at the Bottom
🌍 Atmospheric Pressure
The pressure applied by air (the atmosphere) on the body is called atmospheric pressure. The thick layer of air surrounding the Earth exerts pressure in all directions on everything at Earth's surface. We don't feel it because the pressure inside our bodies balances it. Atmospheric pressure decreases at higher altitudes — less air above means less pressure.

"Force is invisible — you can't see it, only its effects. Yet every movement, every shape-change, every push and pull in the universe is governed by this single fundamental concept."

Exam Preparation

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What is force? What is its SI unit?
A push or pull on an object is called force. Force is a vector quantity (has both magnitude and direction). Its SI unit is Newton (N). Examples of push: kicking, hitting, throwing, pushing, flicking. Examples of pull: picking, lifting, shutting, pulling.
Q2. What is force due to interaction? How is net force calculated?
Whenever there is interaction between two objects, a force acts between them. Net force calculation: (1) If two forces act in the SAME direction: F = F₁ + F₂ (forces add up). (2) If two forces act in OPPOSITE directions: F = F₁ − F₂ (if F₁ > F₂) or F = F₂ − F₁ (if F₂ > F₁). The net force acts in the direction of the larger force. Example: If F₁ = 4N and F₂ = 5N act right, and F₃ = 7N acts left: Fnet = (4+5) − 7 = 2N to the right.
Q3. What are the three effects of force on an object?
A force acting on an object can: (1) Change the shape and size of an object — Example: pressing dough converts a dough ball into a flat chapati. (2) Change the state of motion of an object — Example: pushing a stationary cart makes it move; stopping a moving cart. (3) Change the speed and direction of a moving object — Example: a batsman hitting a cricket ball changes its speed and direction.
Q4. What are contact and non-contact forces? Give examples of each.
Contact forces: Forces that can only be applied by physically touching the objects. Examples: (a) Muscular force — force applied by the action of muscles. (b) Frictional force — force that opposes relative motion between two surfaces in contact. Non-contact forces: Forces that act even without physical touching. Examples: (a) Magnetic force — force exerted by a magnet on another magnet or iron piece. (b) Electrostatic force — force exerted by a charged object on another charged or uncharged object. (c) Gravitational force — force of attraction between any two objects having mass.
Q5. What is pressure? Write the formula and SI unit.
Pressure is the force acting per unit area on a surface. Formula: Pressure = Force ÷ Area. SI unit: Pascal (Pa), also written as N/m². Key rule: Pressure INCREASES when area DECREASES (same force on smaller area = more pressure). Example: A pointed nail pierces a wall more easily than a flat-headed nail because the pointed tip has a much smaller area — so the same force creates much greater pressure.
Q6. Why does a pointed nail go into the wall more easily than a flat-headed nail?
Pressure = Force ÷ Area. A pointed nail has a very small surface area at its tip compared to a flat-ended nail. When the same force is applied to both, the pointed nail exerts much greater pressure on the wall because the area is smaller. Greater pressure means the force is concentrated on a tiny spot, allowing the nail to pierce the wall easily. A flat-ended nail has a larger area, so the same force creates much less pressure — making it harder to push into the wall.
Q7. What are the two factors that affect pressure exerted by liquids and gases?
Liquids and gases exert pressure on the walls of their container in all directions. Two factors affect this pressure: (1) Depth of the liquid or gas — pressure increases as depth increases. At greater depth, there is more liquid above pushing down. (2) Density of the liquid or gas — pressure increases with the increase in density. Example: Why are dam walls thicker at the bottom? Because pressure of water increases with depth — so the bottom walls need to be stronger and thicker to withstand the higher water pressure.
Q8. What is atmospheric pressure?
The pressure applied by air (the atmosphere) on the body is called atmospheric pressure. The atmosphere — the thick layer of air surrounding the Earth — exerts pressure in all directions on everything on Earth's surface. Atmospheric pressure is quite large but we don't feel it because the pressure inside our bodies balances it. Atmospheric pressure decreases as we go to higher altitudes (because there is less air above).
