Types of Minerals

Metallic vs Non-Metallic Minerals

⚙️ Metallic

Metallic Minerals

Contain metals in raw form. Metals are hard substances that conduct heat and electricity and have a lustre or shine.

e.g. Iron ore, Bauxite, Gold, Silver, Copper

🪨 Non-Metallic

Non-Metallic Minerals

Do not contain metals. They are used as raw materials in various industries and chemical processes.

e.g. Limestone, Mica, Gypsum, Coal, Petroleum

Metallic Sub-types

Ferrous vs Non-Ferrous

🏗️

Ferrous Minerals

Contain iron. Most iron & steel industries and heavy industries depend on these minerals. Examples: iron ore, manganese, chromites.

🥇

Non-Ferrous Minerals

Do not contain iron but may contain other valuable metals. Examples: gold, silver, copper, lead. Critical for electronics and precision industries.

Extraction

How Minerals Are Extracted

⛏️

Mining

Taking minerals out from rocks buried under Earth's surface. Two types: Open cast (near surface, dug out) and Shaft mining (deep wells dug down).

🛢️

Drilling

Deep wells are bored to take minerals out. Primarily used for petroleum and natural gas extraction from underground reservoirs.

🪨

Quarrying

Minerals that lie near the surface are simply dug out. Used for stone, sand, gravel, and surface-level mineral deposits.

Conventional — Fossil Fuels

The Fossil Fuel Family

🪨
Buried Sunshine

Coal

Formed from ancient plant matter compressed over millions of years. Nicknamed "buried sunshine" because it stores energy originally from the Sun. The backbone of thermal power generation and steel production.

🛢️
Black Gold

Petroleum

A liquid fossil fuel extracted by drilling deep wells. Called "black gold" for its immense economic value. Used in transport fuels, plastics, and industrial chemicals worldwide.

💨

Natural Gas

A cleaner fossil fuel found alongside petroleum deposits. Used for heating, cooking, and power generation. Produces less CO₂ than coal or petroleum when burned.

💧

Hydroelectricity

Generated from the kinetic energy of flowing or falling water through dams and turbines. A renewable conventional source — clean, sustainable, and widely used in India and globally.

Non-Conventional Sources

Renewable Energy Sources

☀️

Solar Energy

Energy harnessed from sunlight using solar panels. India has great potential for solar energy due to its tropical location and 300+ sunny days per year. Fastest growing renewable sector.

💨

Wind Energy

Generated by wind turbines that convert kinetic energy of moving air into electricity. India has significant wind corridors, especially along coastlines and in Rajasthan and Tamil Nadu.

🌊

Tidal Energy

Harnesses the energy of ocean tides to generate electricity. Predictable and consistent source of power, particularly useful for coastal nations. Still being developed at scale.

⚡

Other Renewables

Include geothermal energy (Earth's internal heat), biomass energy (organic matter), and wave energy. All are renewable, clean, and part of the future energy mix.

☀️

India's Solar Energy Potential

India is located in the tropical zone — receiving abundant sunshine throughout the year. The government has made massive investments in solar farms, and India now ranks among the world's top solar energy producers. With technological upgradation, non-conventional sources like solar are becoming increasingly affordable.

Practice Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What are minerals? How are they identified?
Minerals are naturally occurring substances that have a definite chemical composition. They are formed in different types of geological environments under varying conditions. Minerals can be identified on the basis of their physical properties such as colour, density, and hardness, as well as chemical properties such as solubility. They are distributed in rocks and on the seabed. Tropical regions are very rich in mineral resources.
Q2. What is the difference between metallic and non-metallic minerals?
Metallic minerals contain metals in raw form. Metals are hard substances that conduct heat and electricity and have lustre or shine (e.g. iron ore, bauxite). They are of two types — Ferrous (contain iron: iron ore, manganese, chromites) and Non-ferrous (no iron but contain gold, silver, copper, lead). Non-metallic minerals do not contain metals at all — e.g. limestone, mica, gypsum, coal, petroleum.
Q3. What are the three methods of mineral extraction?
The three methods are:
  • Mining — taking out minerals from rocks buried under the Earth's surface. Two types: (a) Open cast mining — minerals near the surface are dug out; (b) Shaft mining — deep wells/shafts are dug to reach minerals
  • Drilling — deep wells are bored to extract minerals (used mainly for petroleum and natural gas)
  • Quarrying — minerals that lie very close to the surface are simply dug out (e.g. stone, sand)
Q4. Where are minerals found? Name the rock types and regions.
Minerals are found in three types of rocks:
  • Igneous and metamorphic rocks — iron ore, nickel, copper minerals
  • Sedimentary rocks — limestone
In India, the plateau regions — especially the Deccan Plateau and Chota Nagpur Plateau — are among the richest mineral zones. Specific distributions: Iron and Bauxite → Jharkhand, Odisha, Chattisgarh; Mica → Jharkhand, Bihar, Andhra Pradesh (India is the world's leading mica producer); Gold → Kolar, Karnataka.
Q5. What are the uses of important minerals?
  • Iron & Steel — used in every industry; backbone of industrial development
  • Copper — used from coins to pipes; excellent conductor of electricity
  • Aluminium — used in automobiles, airplanes, bottling, building, and kitchen cookware
  • Mica — used in electrical appliances and glass-making industries
  • Silicon — used in the computer industry; obtained from quartz
  • Gems (hard minerals) — used in jewellery making
Q6. How should we conserve mineral resources?
Minerals are non-renewable resources — once used, they cannot be quickly replaced. Key conservation measures:
  • Reduce wastage in the process of mining
  • Recycling of metals — using scrap metal reduces the need for fresh extraction
  • Avoid over-exploitation — which is harmful to both resources and the environment
  • Use minerals judiciously and efficiently in industrial processes
Q7. What are power resources? Why do we need them?
Power resources are sources of energy used to run industries, machines, and services. We need them for:
  • Industry — running factories and machinery
  • Domestic use — cooking, heating, lighting
  • Agriculture — running irrigation pumps
  • Transport — fuel for vehicles, trains, ships
  • Communication — powering networks and devices
  • Defence — running military equipment
Power resources are of two types: Conventional (firewood, fossil fuels) and Non-conventional (solar, wind, tidal).
Q8. What are conventional sources of energy? Name the fossil fuels.
Conventional sources are energy resources that have been in common use for a long time. The two main types are firewood and fossil fuels. Fossil fuels include:
  • Coal — known as "buried sunshine"; formed from ancient plant matter
  • Petroleum — known as "black gold"; a liquid fossil fuel used widely in transport and industry
  • Natural Gas — a cleaner fossil fuel used for heating and power generation
  • Hydroelectricity — generated from flowing/falling water through dams
Q9. What are non-conventional sources of energy? Why are they important?
Non-conventional sources of energy are renewable in nature — they can be replenished naturally and will not run out. Examples: solar energy, wind energy, tidal energy. Key points:
  • They are environmentally friendly — produce little or no pollution
  • They are currently more expensive as they require technological upgradation
  • India has great potential for solar energy — due to its tropical location and long sunshine hours
  • They are the future of energy as fossil fuels are finite and polluting
Q10. Why are mineral-based industries called the backbone of industrial development?
Mineral-based industries are called the backbone of industrial development because:
  • Iron and steel — derived from ferrous minerals — are used in every industry from construction to manufacturing
  • Heavy industries like shipbuilding, machinery, and railways all depend on ferrous minerals
  • Copper, aluminium, silicon power the electronics, aviation, and IT industries
  • Without minerals, no modern industrial economy can function
This is why the mineral-rich plateau regions of India (Chota Nagpur, Deccan) are centres of heavy industry.
