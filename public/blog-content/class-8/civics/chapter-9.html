What Are Public Facilities?

Services That Keep
Society Running

Public facilities are the facilities provided to the people by the government. They are important to sustain and lead a comfortable life. Things like electricity, public transport, schools, colleges, and hospitals — necessary for survival — are known as public facilities. These are services whose benefits must be shared by many people, and no one should be discriminated against in accessing them.

💧
Core Principle · Article 21
Water as a Fundamental Right
The Constitution of India recognises the right to water as part of the Right to Life under Article 21. That means there should be universal access to water. The High Courts and Supreme Court have held that the right to safe drinking water is a Fundamental Right. More than 1,600 Indians — mostly children under five — die every day from water-related diseases like diarrhoea, dysentery, and cholera. Verdicts given by courts make it the legal responsibility of government to ensure the supply of fresh drinking water.

Government's Role
03
State Responsibility
What Must the Government Provide?

The Government's
Core Obligations

One of the most important functions of government is to ensure that public facilities are made available to everyone. Private companies operate for profit — public facilities are related to basic needs and cannot be left to the market alone.

📚
Education
Making provision for education and setting up schools and colleges. Every child deserves access to quality learning regardless of income or social status.
🏥
Health & Sanitation
Improving health and sanitation facilities. Clean hospitals, primary health centres, and sanitation infrastructure save lives and prevent disease.
🌾
Food Distribution
Ensuring equal distribution of food throughout the country. Public distribution systems ensure that no citizen goes hungry due to market failures.
🚌
Transport
Improving the means of transport — buses, trains, roads. Mobility enables citizens to access work, education, and markets.
📮
Public Utilities
Maintenance of public utility works like post offices, railways, and roads — the connective tissue of a functioning society.
How Government Funds Public Facilities
Tax Revenue → Public Welfare
💰
Source of Funds
The main source of revenue is taxes collected from the people. Government is empowered to collect these taxes and use them for public programmes.
🔧
Cost of Water Supply
Pumping water, carrying it long distances, laying pipes, treating impurities, and treating wastewater all require significant infrastructure investment.
⚖️
Affordable Pricing
Government meets expenses partly from taxes and partly by charging a price for water — set so that most people can afford a minimum amount for daily use.
🎯
Goal: Universal Access
The most important task is to ensure availability of resources to everyone. Strict administration and effective scheme implementation can help the underprivileged.
Chennai Crisis
04
Reality Check
Is Water Available to All in Chennai?

The Crisis — and
What Must Change

Chennai's water situation is a mirror of India's public facilities challenge. When government fails to meet its obligation, it is always the poor who suffer most.

The Problem
Chennai's Water Failure
  • Municipal supply meets only about half the city's needs
  • The burden of shortfall falls mostly on the poor
  • Shortage and acute crisis every summer — common to most Indian cities
  • Water shortage is a sign of government failure
  • Industrialisation has added water pollution to water scarcity
The Solution
What Government Must Do
  • Throughout the world, water supply is the government's job — not private companies
  • Technology advancement through government investment can make clean water available to large populations
  • Government must enforce the Right to Life (Article 21) which includes the right to safe drinking water
  • Better administration and effective implementation of schemes for the underprivileged

Exam Prep
Frequently Asked Questions

All You Need to Know

Q1. What are public facilities? Give examples.
Public facilities are facilities provided to the people by the government. They are important to sustain and lead a comfortable life. Examples include:
  • Water supply and sanitation
  • Electricity
  • Public transport (buses, trains)
  • Schools and colleges
  • Hospitals and healthcare
  • Post offices, railways, and roads
These are things necessary for survival, and their benefits are meant to be shared by many people. No one should be discriminated against in accessing them on the basis of social or economic division.
Q2. Why is water considered a Fundamental Right? What does Article 21 say?
Water is essential for life and good health. The Constitution of India recognises the right to water as part of the Right to Life under Article 21. This means there should be universal access to water. High Courts and the Supreme Court have held that the right to safe drinking water is a Fundamental Right. More than 1,600 Indians — mostly children under age 5 — die every day due to water-related diseases like diarrhoea, dysentery, and cholera. Court verdicts make it the government's legal responsibility to ensure the supply of fresh drinking water.
Q3. Explain the three stories of water access in Chennai.
The chapter uses three real-life stories to show how unevenly water is distributed:
  • Mr Ramgopal (Anna Nagar): His area looks lush and green with lawns maintained by generous water spraying — abundant supply for the wealthy
  • Mr Subramaniam (apartment): Faces inadequate municipal supply and spends Rs 500-600 per month buying water
  • Siva (Madipakkam): Gets water only once in 4 days. For drinking, buys bottled water
These stories show that water as a public utility is available in very different quantities to different people — the poor bear the worst of the shortfall.
Q4. What is the government's role in providing public facilities?
One of the most important functions of government is to ensure public facilities are made available to everyone:
  • Making provision for education — setting up schools and colleges
  • Improving health and sanitation facilities
  • Ensuring equal distribution of food throughout the country
  • Improving the means of transport
  • Maintenance of public utility works — post offices, railways, roads
Private companies operate for profit; public facilities are related to basic needs. Government must fund them through tax revenue and use resources efficiently for the maximum welfare of the population.
Q5. How does the government fund public facilities like water supply?
The main source of revenue for government is taxes collected from the people. For water supply specifically, the government incurs costs in:
  • Pumping water
  • Carrying it over long distances
  • Laying down pipes for distribution
  • Treating water for impurities
  • Collecting and treating wastewater
It meets these expenses partly from taxes and partly by charging a price for water. This price is set so that most people can afford a certain minimum amount for daily use. The goal is universal access, not profit.
Q6. What is the water supply situation in Chennai? What does it reveal?
Water supply in Chennai is marked by shortage:
  • Municipal supply meets only about half the needs of the city on average
  • The burden of shortfall falls mostly on the poor
  • Shortage and acute crisis during summer months is common to other Indian cities too
  • A shortage of municipal water is often taken as a sign of government failure
  • Throughout the world, water supply is the government's responsibility — there are very few instances of private water supply
  • In the era of industrialisation, water pollution has become a major additional problem
Q7. Why can't private companies be left to provide public facilities?
Private companies operate for profit in the market. Public facilities are related to people's basic needs — not profitable markets. If water, schools, or hospitals are left to private companies:
  • Only those who can pay will get access
  • The poor will be excluded from essential services
  • This violates the constitutional guarantee of equal access to the Right to Life
  • Universal access cannot be achieved through profit-driven systems
That is why government — funded by taxes from all citizens — must provide these facilities equitably.

The chapter concludes:

  • The major role of government is to ensure adequate public facilities for everyone

  • The success of any government is measured by the facilities and basic needs provided to all its citizens
