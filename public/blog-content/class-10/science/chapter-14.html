Chapter Overview

Where Every Watt Begins

Energy comes in many forms and can be converted from one to another. Drop a plate and its potential energy becomes sound. Light a candle and chemical energy becomes heat and light. But where does the electricity in our homes, the fuel in our vehicles, and the power behind our factories actually come from?

This chapter maps the entire landscape of energy sources — from ancient fossil fuels formed over millions of years, to modern solar cells and nuclear reactors, to futuristic OTEC plants that tap the ocean's warmth. We'll see why no single source is perfect, and why the choice of energy defines the fate of our planet.

02Conventional Sources — Fossil Fuels, Hydro & Biomass
2
The Old Giants

Conventional Sources — Fossil Fuels, Hydro & Biomass

Conventional sources are those humanity has used for decades or centuries — coal and petroleum power thermal plants, dammed rivers generate hydroelectricity, and agricultural waste fuels biogas plants. They dominate our grids today, but each comes with serious trade-offs.
01 · Fossil Fuel
Coal & Petroleum
Fuels formed over millions of years from buried plant and animal remains under heat and pressure. India holds about 6% of world coal reserves — enough for roughly 250 more years at current rates. But burning them releases oxides of carbon, nitrogen, and sulphur, which are acidic and cause air pollution, acid rain, and the greenhouse effect.
02 · Conversion Plant
Thermal Power Plant
Huge amounts of fossil fuel are burnt to heat water and produce steam. The steam is directed onto the blades of a turbine, spinning the rotor. This mechanical motion turns the shaft of a generator, which converts it into electricity. The chain: chemical → heat → kinetic → electrical.
03 · Hydro Power
Hydroelectric Plant
Converts the potential energy of stored water into electricity. Since natural waterfalls are rare, we build large dams across rivers. Water stored high in the reservoir rushes down, turns turbine blades, which drive generators. Around 25% of India's energy requirement comes from hydro.
04 · Biomass
Biogas Plant & Charcoal
Biomass — firewood, cattle dung, crop waste, bagasse — has low calorific value and produces a lot of smoke when burnt raw. Technology fixes this: cow dung becomes efficient biogas (75% methane) through anaerobic decomposition. Charcoal, made by heating wood with limited O₂, burns without smoke and has high calorific value — it strips out the water and volatiles.
Hydroelectric Power Plant
Stored water at height → rushes down the penstock → turns turbine blades → spins generator shaft → produces electricity.
Disadvantages of big dams: Submerging of large areas of flora, fauna, and human settlements. Rotting of submerged vegetation releases methane (CH₄) — a potent greenhouse gas. Dams also pose risks of earthquakes and landslides.
Biogas (Gobar Gas) Plant
Cow dung + water slurry enters the digester. Microbes break it down anaerobically, producing biogas (mostly methane). The gas collects in the dome and flows to kitchen stoves. Spent slurry exits as nitrogen-rich manure.
03Wind Energy & Wind Farms
3
Harnessing Moving Air

Wind Energy & Wind Farms

Unequal heating of landmasses and water bodies by solar radiation creates pressure differences, which generate air movement — the wind. This kinetic energy can lift water from wells or, more importantly, generate electricity. A windmill's rotating blades spin the rotor of a turbine connected to a dynamo.

A single windmill is tiny in output, so many are built together across open land — a wind energy farm. India ranks 5th in the world for wind-power generation; full exploitation of the country's wind potential could produce nearly 45,000 MW of electricity.

Minimum Wind Speed Required
15–20 km/h
Below this speed, the blades don't rotate fast enough to generate useful sustained electricity. Steady winds are critical.
Advantages
1
Eco-friendly — no air pollution, no greenhouse gases, no ash.
2
An efficient renewable source — the wind keeps blowing as long as the Sun heats the Earth.
3
No recurring expenses for fuel — once installed, electricity is essentially free.
Limitations
1
Wind farms need large areas of land — unsuitable for densely populated regions.
2
Difficulty in getting regular wind speeds of 15–20 km/h all year round.
3
High initial cost of setting up a wind farm.
4
High maintenance of blades, exposed to sun, rain, and storms.
04Solar Energy — Cookers, Cells & Panels
4
Non-Conventional Energy · Pt 1

Solar Energy — Cookers, Cells & Panels

The energy emitted by the Sun as heat and light is solar energy. It reaches the outer edge of Earth's atmosphere at a remarkably constant rate — a value we call the solar constant.

Solar Constant
1.4 kJ/s·m2
The amount of solar energy received per second per square metre at the outer edge of Earth's atmosphere. Much of this is reflected or absorbed before reaching the ground.

Solar heating devices exploit two simple physics tricks: black surfaces absorb more heat than white or shiny ones, and glass plates let short-wavelength sunlight in but block the longer-wavelength infrared radiation from escaping — trapping heat inside. This is the greenhouse effect, put to useful work.

Solar Cooker
Box-type cooker: rectangular plastic/wood box, inner walls painted black, covered with a glass plate, with plane mirrors as reflectors to focus rays and raise temperature.
Solar Cell
Directly converts solar energy into electricity. Develops 0.5–1 V and produces about 0.7 W. Made from silicon; silver is used for inter-cell connections.
Solar Furnace
Uses an array of curved mirrors to focus sunlight to a single point, reaching extremely high temperatures — used in industrial heating and materials research.
Solar Water Heater
Water circulates through black pipes inside a glass-covered insulated box. Sunlight heats the water for domestic use — popular in rooftops across India.
Box-Type Solar Cooker
Plane mirror focuses sunlight into the box. Short infrared passes through glass, is absorbed by the black interior, raises temperature; long infrared can't escape — food cooks slowly.

A solar panel is just many solar cells connected together. Panels power everyday electronics — calculators, watches, street lights — and critical systems like artificial satellites, space probes, and remote-area radio & wireless transmission. They need almost zero maintenance, have no moving parts, and don't need a Sun-tracking device, though efficiency is still modest and upfront cost is high.

5
Non-Conventional Energy · Pt 2

Sea, Geothermal & Nuclear Power

Beyond sun and wind, the Earth itself holds vast reservoirs of energy — in the motion of its oceans, the heat of its interior, and the nuclei of its atoms. These non-conventional sources are promising, but each has technical and environmental challenges.

Form 1
Wave Energy
Strong winds across the sea generate waves. The kinetic energy of moving water rotates the turbine of a generator. Limitation: when the wind stops, so does electricity.
Form 2
Tidal Energy
Tides are caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon. A dam across a narrow sea opening traps rising water; turbines at the opening convert the flow into electricity.
Form 3
OTEC
Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion. Needs ≥20°C difference between surface and 2 km-deep water. Warm water boils ammonia; vapour drives a turbine; cold deep water condenses it.
Earth's Inner Heat
Geothermal Energy
Energy stored as heat inside the Earth. Pipes are sunk through holes drilled in the Earth's crust to reach pockets of underground steam. The high-pressure steam rotates turbines of a generator, producing electricity. Found near volcanic regions and hot springs — Iceland and New Zealand run significant portions of their grids on it.

Nuclear Energy — splitting & merging atoms

Nuclear energy is the energy stored in the nucleus of an atom, released during two kinds of reactions.

Nuclear Fission
Splitting a Heavy Nucleus
The nucleus of a heavy atom like Uranium-235, when bombarded with a low-energy neutron, splits into lighter nuclei — releasing a huge amount of energy. This is the process used in nuclear reactors to produce electricity.
¹n + ²³⁵U → fragments + energy + neutrons
Nuclear Fusion
Merging Light Nuclei
Two lighter nuclei (like hydrogen isotopes) join together to form a heavier nucleus, releasing a tremendous amount of energy. This is how the Sun and stars produce energy — and what fusion research hopes to achieve on Earth.
²H + ³H → ⁴He + n + energy

Environmental consequences: Every energy source — renewable or not — has an impact. Use clean fuels like CNG to reduce greenhouse effect. Even manufacturing solar cells causes some environmental damage. Use energy judiciously, and the world lasts longer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good source of energy?
A good source of energy is one that has high calorific value, is easily accessible, is easy to store and transport, and is economical. It should also be safe to use and cause minimal environmental damage on burning or harnessing.
What is the full form of CNG and LPG?
CNG stands for Compressed Natural Gas, and LPG stands for Liquefied Petroleum Gas. CNG is mainly methane (CH₄) stored at high pressure and used in vehicles, while LPG is a mixture of propane and butane used as cooking gas in households.
What is the minimum wind velocity required to obtain useful energy with a windmill?
The minimum wind speed required for a windmill to serve as a useful source of energy is 15–20 km/h (commonly cited as 20 km/h). Below this speed, the turbine blades don't rotate fast enough to generate sustained electricity.
Name the main constituent of biogas.
The main constituent of biogas is methane (CH₄), which forms about 75% of it. Biogas also contains carbon dioxide, hydrogen, and hydrogen sulphide in smaller amounts. It burns without smoke, leaves no residue like ash, and has a high heat capacity — making it an excellent fuel.
Give two examples of fossil fuels.
Two common examples of fossil fuels are coal and petroleum. A third is natural gas. These are formed from buried remains of plants and animals over millions of years under high temperature and pressure, and are non-renewable sources of energy.
Name the device which directly converts solar energy into electric energy.
A solar cell (also called a photovoltaic cell) directly converts solar energy into electrical energy. It produces a voltage of about 0.5–1 V and around 0.7 W of electricity. Solar cells are typically made from silicon, with silver used for connecting cells together. A large arrangement of solar cells connected together is called a solar panel.
What does OTEC stand for?
OTEC stands for Ocean Thermal Energy Conversion. It is a method of generating electricity by using the temperature difference between warm surface water and cold deep-sea water. For an OTEC plant to work, the temperature difference between the surface and water at a depth of 2 km must be at least 20°C. The warm water boils a liquid like ammonia, and the vapour drives a turbine connected to a generator. The cold deep water is then pumped up to condense the vapour back to liquid.
What is nuclear energy?
Nuclear energy is the energy stored in the nucleus of an atom, released during nuclear reactions. It comes in two forms:

Nuclear fission: The nucleus of a heavy atom (like Uranium-235) is bombarded with a low-energy neutron and splits into lighter nuclei, releasing a huge amount of energy.

Nuclear fusion: Two lighter nuclei join together to form a heavier nucleus, releasing a tremendous amount of energy — this is the process that powers the sun.

Nuclear fission is used in nuclear reactors to produce electricity. Major nuclear power plants in India include Tarapur, Rana Pratap Sagar, and Kalpakkam.
Which is a renewable source of energy: solar energy, coal, petroleum, or biogas?
Out of these, solar energy and biogas are renewable sources. Solar energy comes from the Sun, which is virtually inexhaustible, and biogas is produced continuously from organic waste. Coal and petroleum are non-renewable — once used up, they cannot be replenished within human timescales since they took millions of years to form.
Which source of energy would you use to heat your food and why?
A solar cooker or an LPG stove would be ideal choices. A solar cooker is preferred because it uses renewable solar energy, causes no pollution, is economical to run, and can cook multiple foods simultaneously. However, it cannot fry or bake, and cannot work at night or on cloudy days — in which case LPG becomes a cleaner option than burning coal or wood.
State two disadvantages of using fossil fuels as a source of energy.
(i) Air pollution and acid rain: Burning fossil fuels releases oxides of carbon, nitrogen, and sulphur, which are acidic in nature and cause air pollution and acid rain.

(ii) Greenhouse effect and global warming: Large quantities of CO₂ released during combustion trap heat in the atmosphere, enhancing the greenhouse effect and contributing to climate change. Additionally, fossil fuels are non-renewable and will eventually be exhausted.
Write two disadvantages of constructing high-rise dams.
(i) Large areas of land are submerged, destroying the natural habitat of flora and fauna and displacing human settlements. The rotting of submerged vegetation releases methane (CH₄) — a powerful greenhouse gas.

(ii) Big dams pose serious environmental risks such as earthquakes, landslides, and flooding if they fail. They also block fish migration and alter river ecosystems downstream.
Give two limitations and two advantages of a windmill.
Advantages:
(i) It is eco-friendly and causes no pollution.
(ii) It is an efficient renewable source with no recurring costs for electricity production.

Limitations:
(i) Wind energy farms need large areas of land.
(ii) It's difficult to get regular wind speeds of 15–20 km/h, so windmills don't produce electricity continuously. The initial setup cost is also very high and blades require high maintenance.
Name three forms of energy of the oceans that can be converted into usable energy forms.
The three forms are:

(i) Wave energy: Strong winds blowing across the sea generate waves. The kinetic energy of these moving waves rotates the turbine of a generator to produce electricity.

(ii) Tidal energy: Tides are caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon on ocean water. A dam is built across a narrow opening to the sea, and turbines fixed at the opening convert tidal energy to electricity.

(iii) Ocean Thermal Energy (OTEC): The temperature difference between warm surface water and cold deep water (≥20°C) is used to boil a liquid like ammonia. The vapour drives a turbine, and the cold deep water condenses it back.
Explain the principle on which the solar cooker works.
A solar cooker works on the principle of greenhouse effect combined with the absorption of heat by black surfaces. The box is painted black inside because black absorbs maximum heat. A glass plate covers the top — it allows shorter-wavelength sunlight to pass in but prevents the longer-wavelength infrared radiation (re-emitted by the hot interior) from escaping. This traps heat inside and raises the temperature. Plane mirrors act as reflectors to focus more sunlight into the box, further increasing the temperature (typically up to 100–140°C).
Write the advantages and disadvantages of using a solar cooker.
Advantages:
(i) Uses freely available, renewable solar energy.
(ii) Pollution-free — no fuel is burnt.
(iii) More than one food item can be cooked simultaneously.
(iv) Preserves nutrients since cooking is slow and at moderate temperatures.

Disadvantages:
(i) Cannot be used for frying or baking (temperature is too low).
(ii) Cannot be used at night or on cloudy days.
(iii) The direction of the reflector must be adjusted from time to time to keep facing the Sun.
How does a hydroelectric power plant operate?
A hydroelectric power plant converts the potential energy of stored water into electricity. A dam is built across a river to store water at a height in a reservoir. When the sluice gates are opened, water rushes down through pipes and falls with great force on the blades of a turbine. The rotating turbine turns the shaft of a generator (dynamo), which converts mechanical energy into electrical energy. The water then flows back to the river. About 25% of India's electricity requirement is met through hydro power plants.
Why is charcoal considered a better fuel than wood?
Charcoal is better than wood because:

(i) It does not contain water or other volatile materials that are present in wood.

(ii) It burns without smoke and without flame, causing less air pollution.

(iii) It has a higher calorific value than wood, so less fuel produces more heat. Charcoal is produced by burning wood in a limited supply of oxygen.
Why is the solar cooker box covered with a plane glass plate?
The glass plate acts as a one-way trap for heat. It allows shorter-wavelength infrared radiation from the Sun to pass through into the box, but does not allow the longer-wavelength infrared radiation re-emitted by the hot objects inside to escape. This creates a greenhouse effect, causing the temperature inside the box to rise and allowing food to cook. Without the glass plate, heat would escape by radiation and convection, and the temperature would not rise enough to cook.
Why is nuclear fission reaction considered better than burning fossil fuels?
Nuclear fission is considered better because:

(i) Enormous energy release: The fission of 1 kg of Uranium-235 releases energy equivalent to burning 2,500 tonnes of coal.

(ii) Long-lasting fuel: The nuclear fuel is inserted once and produces energy over a long period of time — no constant refuelling needed.

(iii) No air pollution: Unlike fossil fuels, fission does not release CO₂, SO₂ or NO₂, so there is no direct greenhouse or acid rain contribution. However, it produces radioactive waste which must be disposed of carefully.
Why is the use of wood as a domestic fuel not considered good?
(i) Burning wood produces a lot of smoke, which causes air pollution and respiratory problems.

(ii) Cutting trees for firewood leads to deforestation, loss of wildlife habitat, and soil erosion — trees also take decades to grow back.

(iii) Wood has a low calorific value compared to cleaner fuels like LPG or biogas, making it inefficient.
Distinguish between renewable and non-renewable sources of energy. Which is better and why?
Renewable sources (solar, wind, biogas, hydro, geothermal, ocean) are naturally replenished and will last for a very long time — essentially forever on human timescales. They are generally clean and eco-friendly.

Non-renewable sources (coal, petroleum, natural gas) were formed over millions of years and are finite — once consumed, they cannot be replenished within practical timescales. They pollute the environment and contribute to global warming.

Renewable sources are better because they are inexhaustible, pollution-free, and sustainable. However, they currently cost more to set up and may not be reliable in all locations, so a gradual transition is needed.
