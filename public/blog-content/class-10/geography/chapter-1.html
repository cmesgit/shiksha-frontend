Everything that our environment provides us, that is economically feasible, culturally acceptable, and technologically accessible are called Resources.

They form an interactive relationship between nature, technology, and institutions which aid the acceleration of economic development. Human beings themselves are a part of resources — known as human resources.

Overview

How are Resources Classified?

🌱
Basis of Origin
Biotic & Abiotic
Resources from the biosphere (flora, fauna, fisheries) are biotic. Resources from non-living elements like rocks and metals are abiotic.
🔄
Basis of Exhaustibility
Renewable & Non-renewable
Renewable resources replenish naturally (solar, wind). Non-renewable resources like fossil fuels cannot replenish and can be lost forever.
🏛️
Basis of Ownership
Individual, Community, National, International
Resources may be privately owned, community-shared, nationally governed, or managed internationally beyond 200 nautical miles of EEZ.
📊
Basis of Development
Potential, Developed, Stock & Reserves
Resources range from untapped potential to fully developed, or awaiting better technology (stock), or partially utilized (reserves).
Types of Resources

1. On the Basis of Origin

🌿 Biotic

Obtained from the biosphere — includes flora, fauna, and fisheries.

🪨 Abiotic

Obtained from non-living resources such as rocks and metals.

2. On the Basis of Exhaustibility

♻️ Renewable

Can replenish once exhausted. They are naturally restored over time.

e.g. Solar energy, Wind energy

⛽ Non-Renewable

Once completely consumed, they cannot replenish. Must be used with careful planning.

e.g. Fossil fuels

3. On the Basis of Ownership

👤

Individual

Owned privately by individuals.

e.g. Plantations, pasture lands
👥

Community

Accessible to all community members.

e.g. Grazing grounds, burial grounds
🏛️

National

Belong to the nation with legal autonomy. Govt. can acquire private property for public good.

e.g. Mines, forests
🌍

International

Oceanic resources beyond 200 nautical miles of EEZ. No country can use without international permission.

e.g. Open ocean resources

4. On the Basis of Development Status

Potential

Potential Resources

Found in a region but not yet fully utilised. e.g., Rajasthan & Gujarat have enormous wind/solar potential but haven't fully harnessed it.

Developed

Developed Resources

Quality and quantity determined for utilisation. Extent of use depends on technology and feasibility.

Stock

Stock

Available in nature but humans lack equipment/technology. e.g., Hydrogen from water — energy-rich but not scalable yet.

Reserves

Reserves

Technology exists but needs further development. e.g., Hydropower — being generated but limited in full exploitation.

Human Impact

Development of Resources — The Problems

⚠️ Consequences of Over-Exploitation

  • Depletion of resources to satisfy the greed of a few individuals
  • Accumulation of resources in few hands — creating the divide between haves and have-nots
  • Indiscriminate exploitation leading to global ecological crises: global warming, ozone depletion, environmental pollution, and land degradation
The Solution

Sustainable Development

🌱 What is Sustainable Development?

Development that takes place without over-exploiting nature is termed as sustainable development.

Its core principle: "Development in the present should not compromise the needs of future generations."

Key Event

Rio Earth Summit & Agenda 21

📅 June 1992 — Rio de Janeiro Earth Summit

More than 100 heads of state gathered in Brazil for the first International Earth Summit, addressing urgent problems of environmental protection and socioeconomic development.

📜 Declaration Signed

Global leaders signed a declaration on Global Climatic Change and Biological Diversity. The Rio Convention endorsed the global Forest Principles.

📋 Agenda 21 Adopted

Aims to achieve global sustainable development by combating environmental damage, poverty, and disease through global co-operation on common interests and shared responsibilities.

🏘️ Local Agenda 21

A major objective: every local government must draw its own Local Agenda 21 — bringing sustainability planning down to the grassroots level.

Planning

Resource Planning in India

Why is Resource Planning needed?

Resource distribution in India is highly uneven. Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh & MP are mineral-rich; Arunachal Pradesh has water but lacks infrastructure; Rajasthan has solar/wind energy but lacks water; Ladakh has cultural richness but is deficient in water and minerals.

Step 1 — Identification & Inventory

Surveying, mapping, and qualitative/quantitative estimation of resources across all regions of the country.

Step 2 — Planning Structure

Evolving a planning structure with appropriate technology, skills, and institutional setup for implementing resource development plans.

Step 3 — Matching National Plans

Aligning resource development plans with overall national development plans to ensure cohesive progress.

Land

Land Resources & Utilisation

🌲 Forests

Reserved and protected forest areas across the country.

🏜️ Barren & Waste Land

Rocky, arid, desert areas not suitable for cultivation.

🏙️ Non-Agricultural Use

Settlements, roads, railways, factories, and industries.

🌾 Permanent Pastures

Grazing land maintained permanently for livestock.

🌳 Tree Crop Groves

Land under miscellaneous tree crops not counted in net sown area.

🔄 Culturable Waste

Left uncultivated for more than 5 agricultural years.

📅 Current Fallow

Left without cultivation for one or less than one agricultural year.

📊 Net Sown Area

Area sown more than once in an agricultural year. Gross cropped area = Net sown area + area sown more than once.

Degradation & Conservation

Land Degradation & Conservation

🔴 Causes of Land Degradation

  • Mining: Abandoned sites in Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, MP, Odisha leave deep scars and overburdening
  • Overgrazing: Major cause in Gujarat, Rajasthan, MP, and Maharashtra
  • Over-irrigation: Causes waterlogging, increasing salinity & alkalinity in Punjab, Haryana, western UP
  • Mineral processing dust: (cement, ceramic industries) settles on land and retards water infiltration into soil

✅ Conservation Methods

• Afforestation and proper grazing management to control degradation

• Shelter belts of plants and thorny bushes stabilise sand dunes in arid areas

• Proper management of waste lands and control of mining activities

• Treatment and proper disposal of industrial effluents and wastes

Soil

Types of Soil in India

Soil Type 01

Alluvial Soil

  • Found in northern plains — deposited by Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra river systems
  • Also found in Rajasthan, Gujarat, and eastern coastal deltas (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri)
  • Old alluvial = Bangar (kanker nodules); New alluvial = Khadar (more fertile)
  • Rich in potash, phosphoric acid & lime — ideal for sugarcane, paddy, wheat, cereal & pulse crops
Soil Type 02

Black Soil (Regur)

  • Formed from lava flows — ideal for growing cotton (also called Black Cotton Soil)
  • Found in Deccan Trap region — Maharashtra, Saurashtra, Malwa, MP, Chhattisgarh
  • Clayey — retains moisture, rich in calcium carbonate, magnesium, potash & lime
  • Develops deep cracks in hot weather (promotes aeration), sticky when wet
Soil Type 03

Red & Yellow Soil

  • Develops on crystalline igneous rocks in low-rainfall areas
  • Found in eastern & southern Deccan plateau, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, southern Ganga plain
  • Red colour due to iron content; turns yellow when hydrated
Soil Type 04

Laterite Soil

  • Develops under tropical/subtropical climate with alternate wet & dry seasons
  • Acidic (pH < 6.0) — deficient in plant nutrients
  • Found in southern states, Western Ghats (Maharashtra), Odisha, West Bengal, Northeast regions
Soil Type 05

Arid Soil

  • Ranges from red to brown in colour — sandy in texture, saline in nature
  • Lacks humus and moisture
  • Lower horizons occupied by Kanker (calcium content) — restricts water infiltration
Soil Type 06

Forest Soil

  • Texture varies with mountain environment
  • Loamy and silty in valley sides
  • Coarse grained on upper slopes
Erosion

Soil Erosion & Conservation

⚠️ Types of Soil Erosion

  • Gully Erosion: Running water cuts deep channels (gullies). Land becomes "bad land" / ravines (Chambal basin)
  • Sheet Erosion: Water flows as a sheet over slopes, washing away the entire top soil layer
  • Wind Erosion: Wind carries away the fertile top layer of soil

✅ Conservation Methods

  • Contour Ploughing: Ploughing along contour lines slows water flow down slopes
  • Terrace Cultivation: Steps cut into slopes create terraces that retain water
  • Strip Cropping: Strips of grass between crop strips break the force of wind
  • Shelter Belts: Rows of trees planted to block wind — stabilises sand dunes in western India

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. Define resources. Give examples of any two renewable resources.

Everything that our environment provides us, that is economically feasible, culturally acceptable, and technologically accessible are called Resources. Development that takes place without over-exploiting nature is called sustainable development. The core idea is that "development in the present should not compromise the needs of future generations." It aims to balance economic growth with environmental protection for lasting prosperity.

The Rio Convention endorsed the global Forest Principles and adopted Agenda 21 — a plan to achieve global sustainable development in the 21st century by combating environmental damage, poverty, and disease through global cooperation. Q6. Write a note on black soil. Give examples of three states that have black soil.

Black soil, also known as Regur soil or black cotton soil, is formed from lava flows and is ideal for growing cotton. It is found in the Deccan Trap (Basalt) region across the northwest Deccan plateau.

  • Made of clayey material — retains moisture well
  • Rich in nutrients: calcium carbonate, magnesium, potash, and lime
  • Develops deep cracks in hot weather (promotes aeration) but gets sticky when wet
  • States with black soil: Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, and Chhattisgarh (also Saurashtra, Malwa along Godavari and Krishna valleys)
Q7. What are the different types of soil erosion?
  • Gully Erosion: Running water cuts through clayey soil making deep channels called gullies, rendering land unfit for cultivation (called "bad land" / ravines in Chambal basin)
  • Sheet Erosion: Water flows as a sheet over a slope, washing away the top layer of soil across a wide area
  • Wind Erosion: Top fertile soil is carried away by wind. Planting trees (shelter belts) helps reduce this significantly
Q8. Mention the factors that determine land use in India.

Land use is determined by:

  • Physical factors: Topography, climate, soil types
  • Human factors: Population density, technological capability, culture and traditions

Net sown area varies widely — over 80% in Punjab & Haryana, but less than 10% in Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram, Manipur, and Andaman & Nicobar Islands. Forest area is far below the desired 33% as per National Forest Policy (1952).

Q9. "Resource planning is essential for sustainable development." Elaborate.

Resource distribution across India is highly uneven. Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh are mineral-rich; Arunachal Pradesh has abundant water but lacks infrastructure; Rajasthan has solar/wind potential but lacks water; Ladakh is culturally rich but deficient in water and minerals.

Resource planning involves three steps:

  • Identification and inventory of resources through surveying and mapping
  • Developing a planning structure with appropriate technology and institutional setup
  • Matching resource development plans with overall national development goals
Q10. What are the different types of soil found in India?

India has six major soil types: Alluvial, Black, Red & Yellow, Laterite, Arid, and Forest soils.

Characteristics of Alluvial Soil:

  • Composed of sand, silt, and clay in various proportions
  • Classified by age as old alluvial (Bangar) and new alluvial (Khadar)
  • Khadar has finer particles and is more fertile than Bangar
  • Rich in potash, phosphoric acid, and lime — ideal for sugarcane, paddy, wheat, and cereal crops
