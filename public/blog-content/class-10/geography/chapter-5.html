Minerals & Energy Resources

Chapter 5 — Complete revision notes covering types of minerals, ferrous & non-ferrous minerals, conventional energy resources, coal formation, petroleum, and renewable energy sources.

⛏️ Geography📖 Revision Notes❓ 20 FAQs Included

Everything from a tiny pin to a towering skyscraper is made of metal — and metals come from minerals. Earth's crust is composed of minerals, which are the most valuable natural resources usable as fuel and as sources for ferrous and non-ferrous metals.

Alongside minerals, we depend on energy resources — both conventional and non-conventional — which together power India's economic growth.

Mineral

A naturally occurring, homogeneous substance in the Earth's crust with a definite chemical composition and crystalline structure.

Ore

A rock in which metallic minerals are present in concentrated form. Must be refined before metals can be extracted.

Practice Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What is a mineral?

A mineral is a naturally occurring, homogeneous substance found in the Earth's crust with a definite chemical composition and a crystalline structure. Minerals are valuable natural resources used as fuel and as sources for ferrous and non-ferrous metals.

Q2. What is an ore?

An ore is a type of rock in which metallic minerals are present in concentrated form. Ores must be refined or processed before the metals can be extracted from them for practical use.

Q3. What are the main types of minerals?

Minerals are broadly classified into two types:

  • Metallic Minerals — contain metals and are processed to extract them. Further divided into Ferrous (iron-containing), Non-Ferrous, and Precious metals
  • Non-Metallic Minerals — contain no metal but are used for extracting non-metals like mica, sulphur, marble, and limestone
Q4. What are ferrous minerals? Give examples.

Ferrous minerals are those that contain iron. Key examples are iron ore and manganese. Iron ore is the backbone of modern civilization — used from tiny pins to gigantic skyscrapers. Manganese acts as a 'cleanser' in steel manufacturing and is also used in bleaching powder, pesticides, and paint.

Q5. What are the four types of iron ore?
  • Magnetite — highest quality, black in colour, highest iron content
  • Hematite — good quality, reddish in colour, most commercially mined
  • Limonite — lower grade iron ore
  • Siderite — lowest quality iron ore
Q6. Where is iron ore found in India?
  • Odisha — Gorumahisani, Badampahar in Mayurbhanj
  • Jharkhand — Singhbhum, Noamundi
  • Chhattisgarh — Dalli-Rajhara, Bailadila in Bastar
  • Goa — Bicholim, Ratnagiri District
  • Karnataka — Kudremukh in Chikmagalur, Bellary District
Q7. What are non-ferrous minerals? Give examples.

Non-ferrous minerals are metallic minerals that do not contain iron. Examples: Copper — used in electric wires, electronics, and chemical industries. Bauxite — source of aluminium, which is light and resistant to corrosion.

Q8. Where is copper ore mined in India?

Copper ore is mined in Balaghat (Madhya Pradesh), Singhbhum (Jharkhand), and Khetri (Rajasthan).

Q9. What is bauxite and which state leads its production?

Bauxite is a rock consisting of aluminium oxides. Aluminium derived from it is light, resistant to corrosion, and combines the strength of metals like iron. Odisha is the leading producer; important districts include Koraput and Sambalpur.

Q10. What is mica and where is it found?

Mica is a non-metallic mineral with thin, brittle crystal layers that can be split into sheets. It has low power loss and high voltage resistance, making it ideal for electrical and electronic industries. Key mining areas: Koderma and Hazaribagh (Jharkhand), Ajmer (Rajasthan), and Nellore (Andhra Pradesh).

Q11. What are conventional energy resources?

Conventional energy resources are those used on a large commercial scale for generating power over a long time. They include non-renewable fossil fuels — coal, petroleum, and natural gas — as well as running water (hydroelectricity). They are not eco-friendly because they emit greenhouse gases.

Q12. What are the four stages of coal formation?
  • Peat — first stage, lowest carbon content
  • Lignite (Brown Coal) — 30–40% carbon, used in some power plants
  • Bituminous — 40–80% carbon, the most widely used commercial coal
  • Anthracite — ~90% carbon, hardest and highest quality coal
Q13. Where are major coalfields located in India?

The primary source is the Damodar Valley (Gondwana coal). Major coalfields include Jharia, Bokaro, and Giridih in Jharkhand. The Gondwana coal belt also extends through Chhattisgarh, West Bengal, Madhya Pradesh, and Odisha.

Q14. Why is petroleum called 'liquid gold'?

Petroleum is called 'liquid gold' because it has the broadest range of domestic and industrial uses — from fuel for vehicles to raw material for plastics, medicines, and fertilizers. It is formed from dead plants and animals buried in rocky strata on the ocean floor over millions of years.

Q15. What is the regional distribution of petroleum in India?
  • Bombay High — 60–63% of India's total petroleum production
  • Gujarat (Ankleshwar) — about 18–19% of output
  • Assam (Digboi) — India's oldest oil field, about 16% of output
Q16. What are non-conventional energy resources?

Non-conventional energy resources are eco-friendly, renewable, and do not emit harmful greenhouse gases. They include solar energy, wind energy, biogas, tidal energy, and geothermal energy. Being natural 'flow' resources, they are inexhaustible and sustainable — ideal for long-term energy security.

Q17. Where is India's largest solar plant located?

India's largest solar plant is located at Madhapur, near Bhuj in Gujarat.

Q18. Where is the largest wind farm cluster in India?

The largest wind farm cluster is in Tamil Nadu, from Nagercoil to Madurai. Wind energy plants are also concentrated along the coastal areas of Gujarat and Maharashtra due to steady, high-speed coastal winds.

Q19. What is geothermal energy? Where is it found in India?

Geothermal energy is heat energy present in rocks deep within the Earth. Groundwater absorbs heat from these rocks, and when it reaches the surface as steam, it can drive turbines to produce electricity. Parvati Valley near Manikaran in Himachal Pradesh is a key geothermal location in India.

Q20. What is nuclear energy and where are nuclear minerals found in India?

Nuclear energy is released when atoms of one element change into those of another (nuclear fission/fusion). India has uranium deposits in Rajasthan and Singhbhum (Jharkhand), and high-grade thorium in the monazite sands along the coast of Kerala.
